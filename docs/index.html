Don’t save the task. Save your place.

CueBack notices when you step away from work on your phone, keeps your place (the next step, the link, what you had), and hands it back the moment you return.

The problem, and the fix

You didn’t forget the goal. You lost your place.

The problem

Interruptions rarely make you forget the goal. They make you forget where you were: what worked, what failed, and what you meant to do next.

A notification, a call, a friend. Coming back, you scroll, reread and rebuild the thread in your head before any real work happens.

The fix

CueBack keeps your place, not your to-do list.
  • Noticeswhen you step away: the screen locks, or you drift into other apps.
  • Keepsthe app, what you shared to it, and the one next step that matters.
  • Gives back“You’re back in Chrome”, a welcome-back card, and a one-tap Resume.

How it works

One interruption, start to finish.

Real recordings from a Moto g34. A student is working through a physics problem set in Chrome, gets pulled away, and comes back.

1

Save your place

Share the page to CueBack and type the one thing you’d otherwise lose: “Redo 7b, convert km to m first.” Then lock the phone and go.

With automatic detection on, CueBack also notices the pause by itself and asks for the next step only when it isn’t sure.

2

You’re back in Chrome

Open Chrome again and CueBack recognizes the return. A heads-up notification offers your next step, with Resume right there.

Quick glances away don’t count: coming back within two minutes reattaches silently.

3

Welcome back

The welcome-back card leads with what you said you’d do next, and how long ago you paused. One tap on Resume where I left off reopens the exact page.

The card is sized to how long you were away: a light cue after a short break, more detail after a long one.

4

Back in 21 seconds

CueBack times the gap between the welcome-back card and your first stretch of real work in that app. It’s yours to keep, not a score, and Home shows your typical re-entry time.

Any app that shares

A Wikipedia article, a file on GitHub, a lecture on YouTube: each becomes its own place with its own next step.

Every place, kept

Home shows what’s open. The Library searches everything you’ve paused, and any place can be resumed from its detail page.

Set up in a minute

Four short pages: the promise, what CueBack can and can’t see, how you work, and three optional switches.

Under the hood: a pause score, a return score and a recovery depth, all computed on the phone. Details in the setup guide.

Privacy

It keeps your place, and keeps it private.

Cue wears a padlock for a reason. CueBack knows enough to hand your place back, and no more.

Only the apps you pick

CueBack sees which of your chosen apps is in front and when the screen locks. That’s how it knows you paused and when you’re back.

Never what’s on screen

No screenshots, audio, keystrokes, messages, or the content of other apps.

Links only when you share

Links and files are saved only when you share them to CueBack yourself.

Lives on this phone

Your places are stored on the device. Export everything as JSON, or delete everything, from Settings.

Cloud AI is off

Off until you turn it on with a provider you choose. Secrets are redacted and file contents are never sent.

You’re in control

Pause collection at any time, hide details on the lock screen, and set quiet hours for notifications.

Try it

Build it from source.

CueBack isn’t on the Play Store yet. It builds and runs without any keys; billing and push then show a clear “not configured” state.

Build and install

You need JDK 21, the Android SDK, and an Android 9+ phone or emulator.

git clone https://github.com/LSUDOKO/CueBack.git
cd CueBack
cp local.properties.example local.properties
# set sdk.dir in local.properties, then
./gradlew installDebug

Optional keys

  1. RevenueCat: put the public app-specific key in REVENUECAT_API_KEY. A Test Store key (test_…) works in debug builds.
  2. Entitlement: create cueback_pro and attach your monthly, yearly and lifetime products to the current offering.
  3. OneSignal: put the App ID in ONESIGNAL_APP_ID for push. Only privacy-safe tags are sent, never task content.
